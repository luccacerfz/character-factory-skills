# Painted matte miniature workflow

Use this when a generated character looks like smooth plastic, or when making a new character in a painted collectible style. The goal is broad readable color, painted form and edges, and a stable material in the game camera.

## Diagnose the plastic look

Render the same mesh, pose and camera under controlled lighting. Compare the original material under the current lights, diffuse with specular suppressed, and the albedo by itself. A highlight that moves with the viewer comes from the material and lights. One that stays painted in the same place comes from the texture. Start with the stage that introduces the problem.

- Raw PBR inputs can produce broad bright reflections under a strong studio key and rim. They wash out the painted color blocks. For nonmetallic bark, shell and fabric, remove the metallic response, raise the roughness floor and lower specular. A tested Blender starting point was metallic `0.0`, roughness floor `0.88`, Principled specular IOR level `0.055`, and normal strength `0.35`. Tune against the reference and keep deliberate metal on a separate material or mask where the design needs it.
- Light with a broad key and gentle fill. Check rim intensity and reflection probes after reducing the specular response. If the albedo itself contains shiny highlights, repaint those areas or return to a cleaner delit source.
- A matte material can still look flat. Put large diffuse forms, controlled creases, and warm edge accents into the base color. Preserve the approved palette and test the painted pass under the target scene light.

## Make a character in this style

1. **Design at two sizes.** Identify the character's main shape and action, then build the silhouette from a few large masses. Choose three or four colors and a focused accent. Check the face, palette and material boundaries close up, then role and pose at about 64 px. Prepare aligned front, back and side views. Give an articulated jaw room to open, and give limbs clear separation for rigging.
2. **Keep the sculpt and game mesh aligned.** Retain the high-resolution sculpt as your detail source. Retopologize to a device-and-camera budget, clean the UVs, and check the silhouette and dominant features against the concept before binding. Preview the actual game mesh under the same camera; count intentional landmarks such as spikes, stalks or lanterns. Keep separately moving equipment separate when its articulation requires it.
3. **Bake a painted base color.** Start with the delit albedo, keep an untouched copy, and preserve its UVs. The included `paint_bake.py` samples broad AO, fine cavities, curvature and object-space normal from the high sculpt. `tex_paint.py` uses them to paint diffuse shape, warmer edges and cooler shadow accents in linear color. Align the two input meshes in position, scale and orientation, then aim the painted key at the character's actual front. The Warden's paint recipe used `sat=0.94 shadow_sat=0.24 edge=0.24 cavity=0.36 key_hi=1.12 ao_floor=0.47` as tuning for its palette. Begin from the original albedo on every iteration.
4. **Separate animation poses at game size.** Preview Idle, locomotion, guard, attack and defeat from above. If a guard reads like a strike, open a readable gap between cuffs and torso, offset their heights, and give the guard its own weight shift. Use fixed-view silhouette sheets and intersection over union to check pairs that look alike. Probe the full clip for floor and mesh contact, especially a face or jaw moving under a shell or collar. Recheck those frames after export.
5. **Carry the exact finish into the engine.** Export the painted sRGB base color on the original UVs. Import normals and packed masks as data. Apply the roughness floor before converting to engine smoothness, and check actual metallic, smoothness and reflection settings in the prefab. In the Warden's Unity Built-in Standard material, specular highlights and glossy reflections were disabled, with tangent normals kept at strength `0.35`. URP and custom shaders need their own equivalent checks. Keep character effects on separate materials and time them from animation cues.
6. **Prove what the player sees.** Save a same-pose, same-camera material A/B in Blender, then capture Unity Play Mode from the Game camera with the target lights. Review both a close-up and the actual on-screen size. Check that the painted color persists on the LODs, that contacts survive the FBX round trip, and that the silhouette still reads in motion. Record the settings, clips and evidence alongside the asset.

The Warden example used a 1.94M-triangle bake source, a 19.9k-triangle hero mesh and 7.2k/2.7k LODs. It used a shared 2K painted base color across those three meshes. Treat these figures as one validated configuration. Choose targets from the cost, silhouette and quality gates of your own game.
